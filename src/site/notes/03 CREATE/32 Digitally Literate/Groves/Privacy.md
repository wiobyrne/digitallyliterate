# Privacy

Privacy is about who can see information about us, what they can do with it, and whether we have a say in those arrangements. This route treats privacy as a question of power and design, not just a matter of keeping secrets or adjusting personal settings.

The route starts with what it means to build protection into a tool, moves through the defaults that shape everyday use and the institutions that collect and hold information, and ends with questions people can use to challenge a technology decision. It is an opening path through digital privacy, with education and shared services as recurring examples. It is not a legal guide or a complete account of cybersecurity.

## Start here

Read these in order. Each piece moves the question from individual experience toward the decisions and systems that shape it.

1. [[03 CREATE/32 Digitally Literate/Evergreen/Privacy by Design\|Privacy by Design]]: start with the core idea, that a tool should protect people through its structure and defaults instead of leaving safety to each person's settings.
2. [[03 CREATE/32 Digitally Literate/Evergreen/Why Most People Never Change the Default\|Why Most People Never Change the Default]]: see why the default is the decision for nearly everyone, which makes whoever sets it the real decision-maker.
3. [[03 CREATE/33 Newsletter/DL 446\|Nobody Changes the Default]]: see how platform defaults affect young people’s privacy and safety, and why optional settings leave responsibility with users.
4. [[03 CREATE/32 Digitally Literate/Evergreen/Is It a Promise or a Guarantee?\|Is It a Promise or a Guarantee?]]: learn to tell a company's promise not to look at your data from a system that cannot look, and what each one leaves exposed.
5. [[03 CREATE/33 Newsletter/DL 453\|Who Said Yes?]]: follow the consequences of a school data breach involving a vendor families did not choose, and the limits of individual consent in institutional systems.
6. [[03 CREATE/32 Digitally Literate/Evergreen/What Can People Do About AI?\|What Can People Do About AI?]]: use four questions about decision-makers, system access, evidence, and accountability to examine a technology adopted around you.

## Tensions

- **Safety and privacy:** measures intended to protect young people can also require identity checks and create new data risks. [[03 CREATE/33 Newsletter/DL 446\|Nobody Changes the Default]] makes that tension visible.
- **Individual choice and institutional responsibility:** people can change settings, but they often cannot choose the vendors or systems that schools and other organizations adopt. [[03 CREATE/33 Newsletter/DL 453\|Who Said Yes?]] shows the stakes.
- **A promise and a protection:** policies matter, but people also need to know what a system can technically access and what recourse exists when safeguards fail. [[03 CREATE/32 Digitally Literate/Evergreen/Is It a Promise or a Guarantee?\|Is It a Promise or a Guarantee?]] draws that line, and [[03 CREATE/32 Digitally Literate/Evergreen/What Can People Do About AI?\|What Can People Do About AI?]] offers questions for locating those limits.
